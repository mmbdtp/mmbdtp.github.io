<div class="table-container">
      <table>
        <caption>
          Four complementary readings, from biological concepts to genome reconstruction.
        </caption> <thead>
          <tr>
            <th scope="col">Topic</th> <th scope="col">Reading</th> <th scope="col">What to learn</th> <th scope="col">Reading guidance</th>
          </tr>
        </thead> <tbody>
          <tr>
            <th scope="row">Microbiome biology</th> <td>
              <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-020-00875-0">
                Microbiome definition re-visited: old concepts and new challenges
              </a> <span class="citation">
                Berg et al. (2020). <cite>Microbiome</cite>, 8, 103.
              </span>
            </td> <td>
              Distinguish microbiota, microbiome and metagenome. Understand microbial communities as ecosystems, not simply lists of organisms.
            </td> <td>
              Start here. Focus on definitions, community interactions, and ecological concepts such as stability and resilience.
            </td>
          </tr> <tr>
            <th scope="row">Taxonomic profiling</th> <td>
              <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbx120">
                A review of methods and databases for metagenomic classification and assembly
              </a> <span class="citation">
                Breitwieser, Lu &amp; Salzberg (2019; online 2017). <cite>Briefings in Bioinformatics</cite>, 20(4), 1125–1136.
              </span> <span class="citation">
                <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6781581/">
                  Free full text
                </a>
              </span>
            </td> <td>
              Understand marker-based profiling, as used by MetaPhlAn, and k-mer-based classification, as used by Kraken. Distinguish assigning reads from estimating community composition.
            </td> <td>
              Focus on the “Metagenomic classification” section and the discussion of reference databases. Skip the assembly section for this topic. Learn the principles rather than memorising 
              tools.
            </td>
          </tr> <tr>
            <th scope="row">Shotgun metagenomics and assembly</th> <td>
              <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.3935">
                Shotgun metagenomics, from sampling to analysis
              </a> <span class="citation">
                Quince et al. (2017). <cite>Nature Biotechnology</cite>, 35, 833–844.
              </span>
            </td> <td>
              Connect sampling and sequencing to computational analysis. Understand the different roles of read-based and assembly-based approaches.
            </td> <td>
              Read for the overall workflow, then concentrate on assembly-based analysis. Ask what assembling reads makes possible, and what additional challenges it introduces.
            </td>
          </tr> <tr>
            <th scope="row">Metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs)</th> <td>
              <a href="https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.258640.119">
                Accurate and complete genomes from metagenomes
              </a> <span class="citation">
                Chen et al. (2020). <cite>Genome Research</cite>, 30, 315–333.
              </span>
            </td> <td>
              Understand how genomes are reconstructed from metagenomes. Recognise incomplete assemblies, contamination, chimeras, and the importance of genome curation.
            </td> <td>
              Read after the assembly overview. Focus on the evidence needed to trust a reconstructed genome, rather than treating a MAG quality score as a guarantee.
            </td>
          </tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
</div>
